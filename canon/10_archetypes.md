# Book X — Archetypes

## Definition

An Archetype is a persistent ontological structure created from an Idea Entity.

An Archetype is not a Personality.

An Archetype preserves identity, attributes, inheritance, energy, and the ability to manifest Personalities.

## Ontological Stages

Idea Entity
→ Idea Archetype
→ Root Archetype
→ Personality

## Axioms

### A.1

An Idea Entity becomes eligible for archetypization when its existence within Idea Universe reaches 100 percent.

### A.2

Archetypization creates an Idea Archetype.

The original identity of the Idea Entity is preserved.

### A.3

An Idea Archetype is gradually written into Root Universe.

### A.4

When an Idea Archetype reaches 100 percent existence within Root Universe, it becomes a Root Archetype.

### A.5

Only a Root Archetype may accumulate permanent physical Attributes and manifest Personalities.

### A.6

A Personality is a temporary living manifestation of a Root Archetype.

A Personality is not the Archetype itself.

### A.7

Manifesting a Personality consumes most of the currently available Archetype Energy required for that manifestation.

### A.8

A Root Archetype must manifest an appropriate Personality whenever it becomes sufficiently strengthened and reaches its manifestation threshold.

## Attributes

### A.9

An Attribute may exist in one of four states:

- potential,
- active,
- inactive,
- forgotten.

### A.10

A Potential Attribute is known to the Archetype but is not yet sufficiently established to become permanent.

### A.11

An Attribute becomes established when other Idea Entities or Personalities repeatedly observe the originating entity using it.

### A.12

Observation, imitation, learning, and collective recognition strengthen an Attribute.

### A.13

When an Attribute becomes sufficiently established and generally known, it becomes permanently stored in the Root Archetype inventory.

### A.14

A permanent Attribute is never deleted from the Root Archetype inventory.

### A.15

An Active Attribute is currently used by at least one relevant Idea Entity or manifested Personality.

### A.16

An Attribute becomes Inactive when no relevant Idea Entity or manifested Personality continues to use it.

### A.17

An Inactive Attribute remains inherited and available even while unused.

### A.18

A Forgotten Attribute remains permanently recorded in the Archetype but is not normally inherited by newly manifested Personalities.

### A.19

A Personality does not initially know the Forgotten Attributes of its originating Archetype.

### A.20

Forgotten Attributes may be rediscovered through Research.

Research reveals existing forgotten structure; it does not create that structure from nothing.

## Archetype Descent and Combination

### A.21

A new Archetype may descend from one or more existing Archetypes.

### A.22

A descendant Archetype may be created by combining structures and Attributes inherited from multiple parent Archetypes.

### A.23

Attributes inherited from parent Archetypes remain permanently recorded in the descendant Archetype.

### A.24

Inherited Attributes that are no longer expressed by the descendant Archetype are classified as Forgotten.

### A.25

Forgotten Attributes are not normally inherited by Personalities manifested from the descendant Archetype.

### A.26

A descendant Personality may rediscover Forgotten Attributes through Research, experience, or contact with preserved knowledge.

## Archetype Energy

### A.27

A Root Archetype receives energy from the actions of its manifested Personalities.

### A.28

A Root Archetype also receives energy from the actions of its ancestors and descendants when those actions strengthen shared Attributes or identity.

### A.29

Energy may propagate through the Archetype ancestry graph.

### A.30

The energy required to manifest a Personality increases with Archetype Complexity.

### A.31

Archetype Complexity increases with:

- the number of parent Archetypes,
- the number of permanent Attributes,
- the number of combined structures,
- the rarity of its Attribute combination,
- and the depth of its ancestry.

### A.32

Simple Archetypes possess low manifestation thresholds and may manifest frequently.

### A.33

Complex Archetypes possess high manifestation thresholds and manifest rarely.

### A.34

Highly complex Archetypes may require generations of accumulated activity before another corresponding Personality can manifest.

## Consequences

### A.34.1

Common and simple behavioral Archetypes may manifest many Personalities within one generation.

### A.34.2

Rare cultural or artistic Archetypes manifest less frequently because their complexity requires more accumulated energy.

### A.34.3

Exceptional scientific or intellectual Archetypes may manifest only once across many generations.

### A.34.4

The death of a Personality does not destroy its originating Archetype.

### A.34.5

The actions and discoveries of a Personality may permanently strengthen the Archetype from which it originated.

### A.34.6

Civilizations may lose conscious access to an Attribute without removing it from the underlying Archetype.

### A.34.7

Research can restore access to Attributes that remained ontologically present but became Forgotten.
