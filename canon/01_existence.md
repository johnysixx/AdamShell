# Book I — Existence

## Definition

Existence is the canonical domain that defines whether anything may be.

Existence does not determine form, behavior, location, or outcome.

Existence determines whether a concept, entity, event, world, or structure may possess ontological presence within Universe.

## Axioms

### E.1

Existence is contained within Universe.

### E.2

Nothing may enter Reality without first being possible within Existence.

### E.3

Existence precedes Reality.

### E.4

Existence does not select outcomes.

It defines the conditions under which outcomes may become possible.

### E.5

Existence may be partial, distributed, unstable, restored, diminished, or extinguished.

### E.6

An entity may possess different degrees of existence in different Worlds.

### E.7

The total strength of an entity's existence is distinct from the distribution of that existence among Worlds.

### E.8

Movement between Worlds does not necessarily change ontological belonging.

### E.9

Presence within a Layer does not by itself establish existence within the World that contains or exposes that Layer.

### E.10

Existence may be represented by implementation data, but it is not identical to any single field, class, or data structure.

## Corollaries

### E.6.1

A native World may remain the strongest World of an entity even while the entity is present elsewhere.

### E.7.1

`existence_pct` and `existence_by_world` represent distinct canonical concepts.

### E.8.1

`native_world`, `origin_layer`, `current_world`, and `current_layer` must not be treated as synonyms.

### E.9.1

Entering Meeting Place does not automatically redefine an entity's native World or redistribute its existence.

### E.10.1

Dictionary-based and object-based entities may both represent valid forms of existence.
