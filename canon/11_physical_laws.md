# Book XI — Physical Laws

## Definition

Physical Laws are invariant laws governing Physical Reality.

They are not universal laws of the Universe.

They are implementations of one particular Reality.

Different Realities may possess different invariant laws while remaining valid within the same Universe.

## Axioms

### P.1

Physical Reality is governed by measurable invariant laws.

### P.2

Invariant Physical Laws remain independent of individual observers.

### P.3

Physical Laws describe interactions inside Physical Reality.

They do not define Existence, Truth, or Reality itself.

### P.4

Every Physical Law must be internally consistent.

### P.5

Physical Laws may be discovered but are not created by observers.

### P.6

A different Reality may possess different invariant laws without contradicting the Canon.

# Electromagnetism

Electromagnetism is one of the fundamental invariant systems of Physical Reality.

Its current implementation is described by Maxwell Equations.

## Maxwell Equations

### M.1 — Gauss Law for Electricity

Electric charge is the source of electric field.

### M.2 — Gauss Law for Magnetism

Magnetic monopoles are not present in the current implementation of Physical Reality.

### M.3 — Faraday Law

Changing magnetic fields generate electric fields.

### M.4 — Ampère–Maxwell Law

Electric currents and changing electric fields generate magnetic fields.

---

## Consequences

### P.6.1

Physical simulations must satisfy the Physical Laws of their Reality.

### P.6.2

Changing Physical Laws creates a different Reality, not a different Truth.

### P.6.3

The Canon remains unchanged even if multiple Physical Realities coexist.
