# Book II — Truth

## Definition

Truth is the canonical domain of what is.

Truth is not Reality.

Truth does not depend on observation, interpretation, memory, agreement, or belief.

Truth may be represented differently by different Reality Processes without itself becoming multiple.

## Axioms

### T.1

Truth exists within Universe independently of any Observer.

### T.2

Truth is not created by Reality.

Reality processes Truth.

### T.3

A single Truth may produce multiple valid Reality representations.

### T.4

Two Reality Processes may represent the same Truth in mutually opposite ways.

### T.5

Contradiction between Reality representations does not necessarily imply contradiction within Truth.

### T.6

Objective Reality and Subjective Reality are distinct processes.

### T.7

Objective Reality attempts to represent events independently of a particular Entity's internal perspective.

### T.8

Subjective Reality represents Truth as processed through the perspective, memory, knowledge, limits, and state of an Observer.

### T.9

No Subjective Reality is identical to Truth.

### T.10

Objective Reality is not guaranteed to contain complete Truth.

It is the least observer-dependent representation available within the system.

### T.11

Agreement among Observers does not create Truth.

### T.12

Disagreement among Observers does not destroy Truth.

### T.13

Truth may remain inaccessible, partially known, forgotten, distorted, or undiscovered.

### T.14

Research may improve access to Truth but does not create Truth.

### T.15

History records resolved events.

Truth may exceed what History is capable of recording.

## Corollaries

### T.3.1

Multiple Realities may coexist without requiring multiple Truths.

### T.4.1

Opposing interpretations may both preserve different valid aspects of one Truth.

### T.6.1

Objective Reality and Subjective Reality must not be implemented as synonyms.

### T.8.1

Every Subjective Reality belongs to an Observer or a defined collective of Observers.

### T.10.1

Objective Reality must remain revisable when new evidence reveals an incomplete representation.

### T.13.1

An unknown Truth remains Truth.

### T.14.1

Research changes knowledge of Truth, not Truth itself.

### T.15.1

Absence from History does not prove nonexistence within Truth.
